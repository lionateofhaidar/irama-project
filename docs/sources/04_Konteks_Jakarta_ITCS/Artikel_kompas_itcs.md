Imitation Mind Optimized to Reduce Jakarta's Traffic Jam

Imitative intelligence-based technology is optimized to regulate traffic in order to reduce traffic congestion problems in Jakarta.

00:0000:0010101.00xJAKARTA, KOMPAS - Hundreds of billions of rupiah will be disbursed to develop the Intelligent Traffic Control System or ITCS in Jakarta. The artificial intelligence-based technology functions to automatically regulate road intersections so that traffic flows more smoothly. This system is expected to help reduce traffic congestion in Jakarta.

The ITCS has been tested at 20 intersections within the city since 2023. The system utilizes monitoring cameras to record traffic conditions over time.

The footage from the surveillance cameras was then analyzed to adjust the duration of traffic lights according to the density of vehicle flow. Busier road segments will be managed so that the duration of the green light there is extended, allowing for the alleviation of congestion.

The Head of the DKI Jakarta Transportation Agency, Syafrin Liputo, stated that the ITCS is one of the strategic measures in responding to traffic congestion in Jakarta. The preparation of this project was carried out by forming an effective team that utilizes existing facilities or infrastructure in the Traffic Control System Management Unit.

"We are utilizing the existing budget in the pure Regional Revenue and Expenditure Budget (APBD). A total of IDR 120 billion will be allocated for 25 intersections that will be constructed this year," said Syafrin, Friday (4/7/2025).

Overall, the ITCS project is designed in three phases. The short term (0–2 months) includes the signing of a memorandum of understanding for the utilization of ITCS data and the initial operation at five ITCS intersections.

Meanwhile, for the medium term (6–12 months), electronic fines will be implemented, along with enforcement against vehicles that do not pay taxes and do not pass emission tests.

Finally, for the long term (more than 12 months), the development of up to 321 ITCS intersections in Jakarta is targeted. This development includes the integration of systems with low emission zones and electronic traffic control.

The ITCS trial in 2023 is claimed to facilitate monitoring of congestion and smooth 15-20 percent of traffic flow at each point where the system has been installed. Monitoring is carried out from the Network Operation Center Intelligent Transport System Traffic Light or Jakarta traffic control room (Kompas, July 4, 2023).

"ITCS is installed on the inner and outer city perimeters. This is done to read the movement patterns of residents in each perimeter so that congestion policies are targeted accurately," said Syafrin.

On the other hand, several parties believe that traffic congestion in Jakarta has not shown significant signs of reduction. In fact, congestion is considered to be increasing along with the rising number of private motor vehicles.

This matter has been noted by the Strategic Transportation Initiative (Instran), the Indonesian Transportation Society (MTI) Jakarta, the Leaded Gasoline Elimination Committee (KPBB), and the Transport for Jakarta Discussion Forum (FDTJ), which was conveyed to the Governor and Deputy Governor of DKI Jakarta, Pramono Anung-Rano Karno.

Instran noted that in the past 22 years, the number of private motor vehicles in Jakarta has increased fivefold. In 2002, when the busway program was being considered, the number of motor vehicles reached 4.51 million units.

At that time, there were 1.59 million private cars (35.3 percent), 2.44 million motorcycles (54.1 percent), 415,970 freight vehicles (9.2 percent), and 60,997 public transport vehicles (1.3 percent).

However, based on data from 2024, the number of motor vehicles in Jakarta reached 24.35 million units. There are 4.35 million private cars, 19.01 million motorcycles, 44,352 buses, 876,637 freight vehicles, and 64,611 special vehicles.

"This data indicates that the primary cause of congestion is the increasing number of private vehicles. Therefore, the solution to address the congestion issue is to control the movement of private vehicles," said the Chairman of Instran, Budi Susandi.

One alternative to control the movement of private vehicles is by implementing Traffic Demand Management (TDM). TDM includes high parking fees in the city, the removal of fuel subsidies for private vehicles, and restrictions on car ownership only for those who own or control parking spaces.

Another alternative is to restrict the movement of private vehicles through an odd-even system or toll roads, providing incentives for public transportation users, and disincentives for private vehicle users.

MTI Jakarta also underlined congestion as a major transportation issue in Jakarta in the last few decades. All efforts in the transportation sector have been running, including in the provision of infrastructure and allocation of funding, but the capital share or use of public transportation is still around 20 percent.

The Chairman of MTI Jakarta, Yusa C Permana, stated that the efforts to mainstream public transportation have not yet achieved their objectives optimally. Massive improvements in the public transportation sector have also not yielded optimal results compared to the efforts made.

"This reinforces the evidence that improvements in the transportation sector cannot stand alone. Problem-solving must be carried out holistically to address the root of the issues," said Yusa.

One of the suggested ways by Yusa is the establishment of a Single Data system. This is because difficulties in planning, monitoring, evaluating programs, and transportation policies are often related to the absence of centralized data.

According to Yusa, it is time to establish a data bank for all aspects of transportation in Jakarta. This comprehensive data is necessary to facilitate planning and evaluation monitoring from the government’s perspective as well as for public interests.

Data unity will also have an impact on increasing investor confidence in data whose accuracy is guaranteed by the government.

Factions in the DKI Jakarta Regional House of Representatives also highlighted the governance of planning, budgeting, and execution of priority programs, including those related to traffic congestion management. This emphasis is articulated in the general views of the factions of the DKI Jakarta Regional House of Representatives regarding the Draft Regional Regulation on the Accountability for the Implementation of the 2024 Regional Budget.

The Treasurer of the PAN Faction, Syahroni, for instance, questioned the quality of priority spending and the realization figures. The high realization of congestion handling (89.98 percent) should not merely reflect the percentage of budget absorption.

"Has the allocation of Rp 7.15 trillion for traffic congestion management reduced commuting time and logistics costs for small business operators?" said Syahroni.

The PAN faction emphasized the importance of enhancing the capacity for planning, implementation, and oversight of government projects to ensure that every allocated budget can be absorbed maximally and provide a tangible impact for the community.

The Deputy Chair of the Democratic Party and Perindo Faction, Nur Afni Sajim, stated that the provision of integrated and cohesive public transportation services has not been optimal in encouraging the shift from private vehicle users. This is evident from the realization of 22.19 percent of public transport users against the target of 20.97 percent in 2024.

"The achievement of this target is still far from the target of 60 percent of users in accordance with the Regional Regulation of DKI Jakarta Number 5 of 2014 concerning Transportation," said Nur.

The less than optimal achievement is inseparable from the implementation of the push and pull strategy through the development and improvement of public transportation services and traffic engineering management. The Democrat Party and Perindo factions asked for consistency in implementing disincentives for the use of private vehicles.

Has the Rp 7.15 trillion for traffic management reduced commuter travel time and logistics costs for small business owners?

Some of these steps include progressive motor vehicle tax, electronic road pricing/ERP or electronic toll roads, parking tax, vehicle age restrictions, and the implementation of odd-even.

On the other hand, the targets for the MRT phase 3 project of the east-west corridor from Cikarang to Balaraja and the Jabodebek LRT need to be pursued to reach the agglomeration areas. Another step is to enhance the comfort of public transport users through the revitalization of bus stops, expanding the integrated travel information system to all stops, and improving connectivity.
